Scene 09Ten weeks

The curriculum

Ten weeks from "I've used ChatGPT" to a shipped AI project.

No calculus required to start. Real models by week three. Frontier tools by week five. The same arc runs live in the cohort and recorded in Autopilot.

Week 01

How machines learn

Intuition first. Data, patterns, loss, why it works. Students train their first classifier in a browser before the session ends.

Week 02

Python, data and a first real model

Enough Python to be dangerous. Load a dataset, clean it, train, evaluate, argue about the result.

Week 03

Neural networks from scratch

Build a tiny network by hand, then the same thing in PyTorch. The moment the abstraction becomes obvious.

Week 04

Inside a language model

Tokens, attention, transformers, why scale matters. What is actually happening when ChatGPT answers.

Week 05

Frontier APIs and agents

Call OpenAI, Anthropic and Google models. Build a tool-using agent. This is where sponsor products enter the curriculum.

Week 06

Research literacy

Read a paper without fear. Form a question. Design a small experiment. Advisor masterclass on how their lab actually works.

Week 07

Project sprint one

Choose a track: agents, vision, audio, science, product. Mentor groups build. Weekly demos, weekly critique.

Week 08

Project sprint two

Make it work, then make it good. Evaluation, edge cases, cost, safety. Advisor office hours.

Week 09

Write it up

A short paper, a README, a two-minute demo video. Ethics and limits, written by the student, in their own words.

Week 10

Demo day

Present to advisors, parents and invited partners. Top projects go to the Think ML Journal and the Fellows track.

Scene 14What could go wrong

Risks and moats

The honest list, and the answer to each.

"Anyone can launch an AI course for teens."

True. Bootcamps, tutors and YouTube will all try.

Moat

Advisors, outcomes and a brand parents already trust. Courses are commodities. A program the labs judge is not.

The curriculum goes stale in six months.

The field moves monthly. Any recorded product decays.

Moat

Autopilot is re-recorded from every live cohort. The live track is the refresh mechanism, paid for by tuition.

Advisors do not sign.

Big names are busy and their employers are careful.

Moat

Think Neuro has done this before. The ask is small and the pool is huge: alumni parents, mentors' former colleagues, recruiters who want the pipeline.

Parents think AI is cheating.

Some schools ban it. Some parents fear it.

Moat

We are the safe, supervised, credentialed path. Position against misuse, not around it. Fear is the reason they buy.

The live cohort does not scale.

Mentors, timezones and quality all cap growth.

Moat

It does not have to. The cohort is the brand and the content engine. Autopilot and sponsorship are the scale.

Dilutes the Think Neuro brand.

A weak launch reflects on the parent company.

Moat

Launch small, under the brand, with the same quality bar. The pilot is thirty students and one demo day. Prove it, then push.
